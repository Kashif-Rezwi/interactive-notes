1. Mathematical Foundations & Notation

Scalar

A single numerical value. $a \in \mathbb{R}$

ML Examples: Learning rate, weight value, bias term, loss value.

Vector

An ordered 1D array of numbers. $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$

$$x = \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ \dots \\ x_n \end{bmatrix}$$
ML Examples: Feature vectors, word embeddings.

Matrix

A 2D arrangement of numbers. $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$$
ML Examples: Datasets (Rows=Samples, Cols=Features).

Functions in Machine Learning

A function maps inputs to outputs: $f : \mathbb{R}^n \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$. In a linear model, this is expressed as:

$$f(x) = w^T x + b$$

Where $w$ are weights, $x$ is the feature vector, and $b$ is the bias. Every neural network layer is a sequence of these linear transformations followed by non-linear activations.

Summation notation $\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i$ is extensively used in loss functions and gradients.

Proof: Linearity of Dot Product

Prove that $(x+y) \cdot z = x \cdot z + y \cdot z$

$$(x+y) \cdot z = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_i + y_i)z_i$$ $$= \sum_{i=1}^{n} (x_i z_i + y_i z_i)$$ $$= \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i z_i + \sum_{i=1}^{n} y_i z_i$$ $$= x \cdot z + y \cdot z$$

Such derivations are extremely important in gradient derivations and backpropagation proofs.

2. Vector Spaces & Geometry

A vector space is a structure where vector addition and scalar multiplication are valid. The concepts of Linear Combination, Span, and Basis are central to latent embeddings and PCA.

Linear Combination

Formed by scaling vectors and adding them together: $a_1 x_1 + a_2 x_2 + \dots + a_k x_k$. Every prediction in a linear model is a weighted linear combination of features.

Span & Basis

The span is the set of all possible linear combinations. A basis is the minimum set of linearly independent vectors required to span a space.

The Dot Product

The dot product measures similarity between vectors.

Algebraic
$$x \cdot y = \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i y_i$$
Geometric
$$x \cdot y = \|x\| \|y\| \cos \theta$$

Norms

L2 Norm (Euclidean)

$$\|x\|_2 = \sqrt{\sum x_i^2}$$

Measures straight-line distance. Used in Gradient descent, Ridge Regularization, and distance metrics.

L1 Norm (Manhattan)

$$\|x\|_1 = \sum |x_i|$$

Promotes sparsity. Used in Sparse models, Lasso Regression, and Feature selection.

4. Projections & Least Squares Extremely Important

Given a system $Ax=b$, an exact solution often does not exist due to noise or more equations than unknowns. Least Squares finds the best approximate solution by minimizing squared error.

Normal Equation
$$x = (A^T A)^{-1} A^T b$$

5. Matrices & Transformations

Matrix Multiplication

$$C = AB$$

Combines transformations. It is not commutative ($AB \neq BA$). Order matters significantly in ML pipelines.

Matrix-Vector Product

$$Ax$$

Applies a linear transformation (rotation, scaling, projection). Neural network layers internally perform repeated matrix-vector multiplications.

Identity & Transpose

$$Ix = x \quad (A^T)_{ij} = A_{ji}$$

Transpose swaps rows/cols, vital for Covariance and Backpropagation.

Symmetric Matrix

$$A = A^T$$

Covariance matrices are symmetric. They have real eigenvalues and orthogonal eigenvectors (essential for PCA).

6. Rank & Linear Independence

Linear Independence

Vectors are linearly independent if:

$$a_1 x_1 + a_2 x_2 + \dots + a_n x_n = 0 \implies a_i = 0$$

Meaning: No vector can be represented using others. No redundancy exists.

Rank

Rank is the number of linearly independent columns (or rows) in a matrix.

  • Full rank: no redundancy
  • Low rank: redundant features (Multicollinearity problem)

PCA specifically aims to reduce the rank of data representations by finding the most important independent components.
