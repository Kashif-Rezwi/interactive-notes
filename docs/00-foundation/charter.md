# Learning OS Charter

## Vision

Technical knowledge should be explorable, accurate, accessible, and memorable—not merely displayed. Learning OS will enable people and organizations to transform trusted learning material into interactive educational experiences with evidence-backed AI workflows.

## Mission

Build a durable, agent-readable operating system that coordinates the future creation, evaluation, revision, and stewardship of learning experiences.

## Product boundary

Learning OS will eventually orchestrate transformations from source material—such as notebooks, papers, PDFs, slides, Markdown, documentation, books, and technical articles—into lessons, explorable explanations, visualizations, diagrams, quizzes, flashcards, concept maps, cheat sheets, revision notes, and study guides.

This repository does **not** build that application. Its present deliverable is the knowledge system and governance that make a future implementation trustworthy and repeatable.

## Primary users

- Learners seeking understanding rather than passive summaries.
- Educators and domain experts who need controllable, reviewable teaching artifacts.
- AI agents and engineers who need precise context and safe handoffs.
- Organizations requiring provenance, evaluation, and responsible content operations.

## Success measures

- A capable agent can begin bounded work by reading repository documents alone.
- Every important output can be traced to its source, plan, prompt, model, evaluation, reviewer, and revision history.
- Quality improves across runs through curated memory rather than unexamined retries.
- The repository can grow from solo experimentation to production governance without replacing its conceptual model.

## Non-goals for this phase

- Building a web application, runtime, model gateway, API, or command-line tool.
- Choosing a frontend framework, rendering library, database, cloud, or model vendor as an implementation commitment.
- Claiming learning efficacy without a defined evaluation design.

## Invariants

1. **Traceability:** outputs and decisions retain their lineage.
2. **Pedagogical honesty:** uncertainty, assumptions, and approximations are visible.
3. **Human accountability:** AI may propose and execute bounded work; humans own releases and high-impact decisions.
4. **Accessibility by design:** accessibility is a quality dimension, not a late-stage repair.
5. **Reversible learning:** experiments are isolated, recorded, and can be superseded without erasing their evidence.
6. **Least premature commitment:** defer irreversible technology choices until they have requirements, alternatives, and acceptance criteria.
