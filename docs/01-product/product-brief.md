# Product Brief

## Problem

Technical learning materials often contain accurate information but poor learning affordances: hidden prerequisites, untested misconceptions, passive consumption, inaccessible diagrams, and no path from theory to practice. Producing high-quality alternatives is slow and requires multiple disciplines.

## Value proposition

Learning OS will coordinate AI and human expertise to convert authorized source material into evidence-grounded educational artifacts that make concepts observable, manipulable, assessable, and revisable.

## Artifact taxonomy

| Family | Purpose | Minimum learner value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interactive lesson | Guide a sequenced learning journey | Outcomes, explanation, activity, feedback, and retrieval practice |
| Explorable explanation | Let learners manipulate a model | Meaningful controls, visible causal change, and interpretation guidance |
| Visualization/diagram | Make relationships perceptible | Accurate encoding, labels, text alternative, and cited assumptions |
| Assessment | Surface understanding and misconceptions | Aligned objective, rationale, valid answer logic, and feedback |
| Revision asset | Support recall and synthesis | Compactness, source-grounding, and links to deeper learning |

## Core learner outcomes

An artifact should help a learner explain a concept, recognize when it applies, predict outcomes, practice a procedure, diagnose a misconception, or transfer knowledge to a related problem. “Looks polished” is never sufficient.

## Boundaries and safeguards

- Transform only material with an approved transformation basis.
- Cite or link source claims according to the provenance policy.
- Clearly label generated examples, simplifications, synthetic data, and uncertain statements.
- Do not infer a learner’s ability, identity, or educational need without consent and clear purpose.
- Design for diverse access needs from the teaching plan onward.
