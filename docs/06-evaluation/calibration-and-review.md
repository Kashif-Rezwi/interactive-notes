# Calibration and Human Review

## Evaluator calibration

Before a rubric or evaluator is used for release decisions, score a shared calibration set. Compare score distributions and rationale quality against an adjudicated reference. Recalibrate after material rubric, prompt, domain, or artifact-family changes.

Track agreement by dimension, not only aggregate score. Low agreement often signals an ambiguous rubric, insufficient evidence, an underspecified audience, or evaluator bias—not simply poor evaluator performance.

## Review assignments

| Condition | Required reviewer |
| --- | --- |
| Any instructional artifact | Educational reviewer |
| Mathematical/scientific claims | Appropriate domain reviewer |
| Interaction or visual model | Experience and visualization reviewer |
| Learner-facing artifact | Accessibility reviewer |
| Rights/privacy/safety concern | Human accountable owner and relevant specialist |
| Release-critical disagreement | Independent adjudicator and release steward |

## Adjudication

Adjudication preserves original evaluations, names the disputed claims, reviews source evidence and rubric language, and records the decision plus residual uncertainty. It may revise the candidate, rubric interpretation, benchmark case, or workflow—but each change has its own traceable record. Do not overwrite disagreement to create artificial consensus.

## Human override

Humans may accept residual risk only with a written rationale, scope, time limit, mitigation, and review date. An override never changes the underlying score or erases the defect; it changes the release decision and creates a follow-up obligation.
